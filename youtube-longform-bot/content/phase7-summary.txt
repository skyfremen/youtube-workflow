Working title: Why Does Laundry Never Feel Finished?

Approximate intended length: 30–45 seconds

Story overview:
Laundry feels endless because it is not really a one-time task. The moment one load is done, more clothes are already being worn, towels are being used, and another pile starts forming. The video explains this as a continuous system rather than a finishable chore, then turns the missing-sock problem into the comic payoff.

Scene 1 — The false finish:
We open in a laundry room just after a load is finished. The character thinks the job is done, but another laundry basket appears and immediately destroys that feeling of victory.

Scene 2 — Why the cycle restarts:
The washing machine keeps running while the character is still wearing clothes. Simple animated labels show wear → wash → repeat, making the point that laundry keeps producing new work while old work is being completed.

Scene 3 — Empty basket versus reality:
The basket finally looks empty and the character gets a brief success moment. A few moments later, another towel or random item appears, followed by one mysterious sock.

Scene 4 — The missing sock returns:
The character has already accepted that the missing sock is gone forever. The sock suddenly comes back with an exaggerated “I'M BACK” moment, giving the middle of the story a visual joke.

Scene 5 — Final explanation and punchline:
We return to the full laundry-room scene. The takeaway is that laundry is a system, not a finish line. The final joke is that laundry is basically a subscription service your clothes signed you up for without asking.

Main punchline/payoff:
Laundry is not something you finish — it is a subscription service your clothes enrolled you in forever.
